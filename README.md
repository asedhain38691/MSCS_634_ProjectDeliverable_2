# MSCS_634_ProjectDeliverable_2

For this project, I worked with an employee salary dataset containing 375 records and six key attributes: Age, Gender, Education Level, Job Title, Years of Experience, and Salary. After cleaning the data by removing the few rows with missing values and standardizing categorical fields, I conducted exploratory data analysis to examine distributions, relationships, and trends. This revealed that Salary is strongly influenced by Years of Experience and Education Level, and that certain job titles cluster around higher pay ranges. These insights guided my feature engineering choices, where I encoded categorical variables appropriately: LabelEncoder for Gender, OrdinalEncoder for Education Level due to its inherent ranking, and OneHotEncoder for Job Title to avoid introducing false order.

Next, I built two regression models: Linear Regression and Lasso Regressionto predict employee salary. Both models were trained on the processed dataset and evaluated using R², MSE, RMSE, and 5-fold cross-validation. Linear Regression achieved an R² of 0.8246 with an RMSE of 20,503, while Lasso Regression performed slightly better with an R² of 0.8271 and an RMSE of 20,361. Cross-validation reinforced this result, with Linear Regression averaging 0.8384 and Lasso Regression averaging 0.8408, indicating strong generalization and stability. The improvements from Lasso, though small, suggest that regularization helped by reducing the influence of less significant job title categories.

During the process, I faced challenges involving inconsistent categorical values (such as apostrophes in education levels) and the need to choose the correct encoding technique for each feature. These were resolved by cleaning the text fields and applying the correct encoders based on whether categories were ordinal or nominal. Another challenge was avoiding model bias from outliers; I addressed this by analyzing residuals and confirming that both models were stable without excessive distortion.

Overall, the modeling process demonstrated that salary prediction can be effectively performed using simple regression techniques, and that even small improvements from regularization contribute to better model performance. Lasso Regression emerged as the best model, offering slightly higher accuracy and better generalization across folds. This project provided a complete end-to-end experience in data cleaning, feature engineering, modeling, and evaluation while delivering meaningful insights into the factors that influence employee salary.
